Overview

Why MakeMyTrip

MakeMyTrip is one of India’s biggest travel booking platforms. For the Principles Project at RMIT I picked it, along with Shein, found real usability problems on each, tested them, redesigned them and tested again.

This case study covers MakeMyTrip. I found three problems: a search form that tries to show everything at once, fees that show up late, and no obvious way to compare flights.

Problem

Three things that got in the way

01
An overwhelming booking flow

The search form is crowded with trip type, fare type, a GST field, price drop protection and promos, all competing with the main action.

02
Hidden fees

Fees showed up late, so the price people saw first wasn’t always the price they ended up paying.

03
Comparing flights

It wasn’t obvious where to click to compare flights side by side.

Research

What testing the original showed

5-second test

I started with a 5-second test on the original page with 15 people on Lyssna. They see the page briefly, then say what they remember. It shows what a page communicates before anyone has time to read it.

0/15recognised it as a flight booking page
0/15remembered trip details like the route, dates or the cheapest and fastest tags
0/15remembered the clutter, like smart filters or price protection

So the page says what it is. Almost everyone remembered the trip itself, and hardly anyone remembered the features crowding the search form.

First click test

I then asked 15 people on the original page what they were looking for when they clicked, after being asked where they would click to compare flights.

0/15were looking for a way to compare flights, like times, prices or all the options
0/15expected clicking the price to show more options
0/15had no clear goal, such as choosing a destination or working out what the site is

In think-aloud tests on the original site, people took 7:19, 5:21 and 6:12 to finish the booking task. Most people knew what they wanted, but nothing on the page showed them how to get it.

What I took from it

The page was clear about what it was. It was the amount competing for attention that slowed people down.

Redesign

Fixing each problem, then testing again

For each problem I redesigned the screens in Figma and ran the same kind of test again on Lyssna, so I could compare before and after. Pick a problem, then drag the slider to compare.

Results

Did it get better?

Faster to finish the task

Time on task in the think-aloud tests dropped for every participant. These times are approximate.

Faster to find the right click

In the first click test, 10 of 15 people clicked the right place on the redesign, and the average time dropped from 23 seconds to 12. On the original, 10 of 15 were looking for a way to compare flights, but nothing showed them where it was.

Hidden fees

All 3 participants said the final price matched what they expected, and rated their confidence 4, 5 and 4.

Tests I ran

5-second test on Lyssna with 15 responses on the original and 17 on the redesign. First click test with 15 on each. Think-aloud with 3.

Outcomes

What changed for each problem

Problem 1

Scattered → focused

Attention moved from the dates and filters to the flight. All 15 still knew what the site was for.

Problem 2

6:17 → 3:15

Average time on task. Price confidence rose from 2.7 to 4.3 out of 5, and 3 of 3 said the final price matched.

Problem 3

23s → 12s

Time to click. 10 of 15 knew where to click to compare flights.

Takeaway

Taking noise out and making the key action or price obvious helped people reach their goal faster and with more confidence.

Reflection

What I take from this

What went well

Every redesign decision traced back to a problem I had recorded and a principle behind it, and I tested each one with numbers.

What I learned

Every design choice needs a reason. Krug, Nielsen and Johnson gave me one for each change, so I removed noise and made the key action obvious.

What I would change

Test with more people, use the same number of testers for the original and the redesign, and test the compare feature on mobile.
