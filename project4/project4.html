Overview

A photo strip you decorate by hand

Context

A fun photobooth style strip maker where you can upload your everyday photos and the site turns them into a photo booth strip.

Action: drag and drop

You can add stickers and change the colour of the frames to customize them.

What the finished booth does

Upload up to three photos

Click a photo box to add a picture. Scroll or use the plus and minus buttons to zoom, and drag to reposition inside the box.

Drag or tap to decorate

Frame colours and stickers can be dragged onto the strip, or just clicked.

Edit placed stickers

Once a sticker is on the strip you can move it, resize it, rotate it and delete it.

Download as a PNG

The strip is redrawn on a canvas for the download, so the saved image matches what is on screen.

Sticker packs

A dropdown with a trial pack and three locked packs, as a mock up of how a bigger site could unlock extra packs with a subscription.

Research

Three tools that do something similar

I looked at how existing sites handle photos, frames and stickers before sketching my own. Click an image to enlarge it.

Example 1

Kapwing

A full editor. You start with an upload box, a blank canvas and a long tool column down the side.

Example 2

Photobooth.io

Focused on strips. You change the layout, reorder photos and pick a frame colour or pattern from a big grid.

Example 3

IdPhotoDiy

Built around stickers. You upload a photo, then use Add Sticker and Delete Sticker buttons and a size setting.

Reasoning

How the action fits

01
It feels like a real strip

It's a fun photo booth website, so the interaction should feel like playing with a real photo strip.

02
It's how you'd decorate one

Drag and drop fits because it's how you'd naturally decorate a strip.

03
Straight to decorating

Photos come already loaded, like a tutorial, so users can go straight to decorating.

04
Learn it once

Stickers and colours use the same action, so people only have to learn it once.

05
Focused first plan

To keep the first prototype focused, I planned for the upload button not to work. That changed later.

06
Two drags

Pick up a sticker and place it on the strip. Drag a colour onto the frame to change it.

The mock up keeps the sketch's three columns: frame colours on the left, the strip with its photos in the middle, stickers on the right.

Plan versus build

The project grew past the plan

My presentation scoped a small prototype: pre-loaded photos, draggable stickers and colours, and a dead upload button. While building, I kept adding what I would want as a user.

In the plan

Photos pre-loaded on the strip. Stickers and colours dragged on. Upload button present but not working.

In the final build

Working upload for up to three photos, zoom and reposition, movable and resizable stickers, a PNG download and a locked sticker pack menu.

Decisions I can point to

01
Cropping you control

Photos come in all shapes, so each one can be zoomed and moved inside its box instead of being cut off automatically.

02
Download matches the screen

I built the download with the canvas so people get the strip they designed, not an approximation.

03
Feedback in the hint line

The instruction text at the top doubles as the feedback line. After a download it changes to say it saved.

04
Drag or click

Dragging is the main idea, but every colour and sticker also works with a click for people who do not want to drag.

How I used AI

I used Claude when I got stuck, mostly to explain things like how to add the upload and download functions, how to make uploaded images zoom and reposition, and how to centre the strip between the side panels. I reviewed and approved every suggestion before using it, and the ideas and decisions above are mine.

Bigger picture

One small part of an events site

This prototype is one small part of a bigger website for an events company that plans weddings, birthdays, graduations and other events.

On the full site, customers can customise the stickers and frames to suit their event. For this project I'm only focusing on one piece of it, the photo booth strip, and the drag and drop interaction that goes with it.
